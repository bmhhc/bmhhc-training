Agency Administration, Supervision, Management and Oversight

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. has a Board of Directors that functions as the governing body and has full legal authority and responsibility for the operation of the agency. The Board appoints an Agency Administrator, who is responsible for agency management and oversight and for employing a care supervisor who is available during normal agency operating hours (Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.).

Purpose

To state who is responsible for running the agency and for supervising direct care workers.

Procedure

  1. The Board of Directors is responsible for:
    • Appointing a qualified Administrator of Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc.
    • Adopting and annually reviewing the Board of Directors bylaws.
    • Meeting at least once per year to oversee the management and fiscal affairs of the agency, including approving the annual budget.
  2. The Administrator is responsible for the overall organization, planning, delivery and evaluation of the agency's services, and keeps ongoing communication among the Board of Directors, agency staff and the care supervisor.
  3. The Administrator ensures that:
    • Qualified personnel are employed, using methods such as online job postings, newspaper advertising and referrals from current staff.
    • Staff receive adequate education, training and evaluations.
    • Public information materials are accurate.
    • An effective budgeting and accounting system is kept in place.
  4. The care supervisor oversees the day-to-day work of direct care workers, including the development of worker qualifications, the assignment of workers to consumers, and follow-up on consumer and family concerns.
  5. After hours, on weekends and on holidays (including 4:30 p.m. to 9:00 a.m. each day), the on-call system described in AO.003 is used so that a supervisor can always be reached. The on-call schedule is updated regularly based on the availability of qualified supervisory staff and the needs of consumers.
  6. Delegation of authority: in the absence of the Administrator, the care supervisor, or another person the Board names in writing, acts for the Administrator.
  7. Any change in the Administrator, ownership or governing body is reported within thirty (30) days to the Pennsylvania Department of Health and to any other agency or payer that requires notice, including UPMC and the Office of Long-Term Living. Policy: AO.003

On-Call Course of Action

Policy

It is the policy of this agency that consumers, families and direct care workers can reach a supervisor after hours to ask questions, report a problem or arrange coverage.

Purpose

To make sure someone is always available when a consumer's care is affected outside normal office hours.

Procedure

  1. The on-call system is not an emergency response system. Consumers and families are told at the start of services, and again as needed, to call 911 for any emergency and to call the on-call number for schedule or care questions.
  2. After hours, on weekends and on holidays, calls are answered by the answering system, which only routes the call. The on-call supervisor decides how serious each call is and how it will be handled.
  3. The on-call supervisor returns calls promptly and decides whether to send a replacement worker, call the consumer's family or emergency contact, or contact emergency services.
  4. If a consumer is admitted to the hospital or dies, the on-call supervisor notifies any worker scheduled to visit so that no unnecessary visits are made.
  5. If a worker cannot cover a shift, the on-call supervisor arranges a back-up worker (see AO.004) or notifies the consumer and the care supervisor first thing the next business day.
  6. The on-call supervisor records each call, the action taken and any follow-up needed, and gives the record to the care supervisor the next business day.
  7. The Administrator or care supervisor may change the on-call procedure as needed to meet consumers' needs.

Policy: AO.004

Back-Up Services / After-Hours Care

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. keeps systems in place so that back-up services are available to consumers when the assigned direct care worker or contractor cannot provide the scheduled service.

Purpose

To make sure a consumer's care is covered when the assigned worker is unavailable, and that consumers know how to reach the agency after regular business hours.

Procedure

  1. The Administrator or designee reviews staffing levels on a regular basis to make sure the agency can meet the needs of its consumers.
  2. If adequate staffing is not available, or is expected not to be, the shortage is met by securing qualified staff through an arrangement with another agency or a supplemental staffing company.
  3. Anyone who provides care under such an arrangement completes orientation to the policies and procedures of the agency, and meets the hiring and competency requirements of HR.001 and HR.004, before being assigned to a consumer.
  4. Back-up services may be provided by agency staff, contractors, or the consumer's family members and friends.

After-Hours Care

Each consumer is told, at admission, how to reach the agency after regular business hours (see AO.003).

Designee

If the consumer has selected a designee, the designee must be willing and able to provide the necessary services. If the designee agrees to provide back-up services, the agency has the designee sign a written agreement (the Back-Up Services Designee Agreement that follows this policy) to serve as the back-up service provider. The signed agreement is kept in the consumer's client record.

Communicating with Limited English Proficient (LEP) Consumers

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. takes reasonable steps so that people with limited English proficiency (LEP) have an equal opportunity to take part in its services, activities, programs and other benefits. The agency ensures effective, meaningful communication with LEP consumers and their authorized representatives about their needs, services and benefits. This includes vital documents such as consumer rights, consent forms, and financial and insurance benefit forms. Interpreters, translators and other aids are provided at no cost to the person served. All consumers and families are told that this help is available. Staff who may have direct contact with these consumers are trained in effective communication, including the use of interpreters.

Purpose

To make sure language is not a barrier to receiving services or understanding rights.

Procedure

  1. Consumers, families and interested persons with an LEP barrier are offered interpreter service by the agency, at no cost to them, to support communication and equal access to services while services are being provided.
  2. When providing care to LEP consumers and families:
    A.Determine whether the person has LEP and, if so, which language is spoken.
    B.A family member or friend may serve as interpreter only after the person has been told of the right to free interpreter services and has chosen to use that person.
    C.Interpreter services are available at no cost to the consumer through the agency's telephone language line, 1-800-752-6096. [Confirm the current vendor and number.]
    D.The consumer's choice of interpreter or interpreter service, and the presence of an interpreter, is documented in the client record after every visit, including the discussion, the need for an interpreter, the interpreter's name, and whether the offer was accepted or refused.

Policy: AO.007

Communicating with Visual, Hearing or Speech Impaired Consumers

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. provides services to consumers who are visually, hearing or speech impaired. Needed services and accommodations are provided at no charge to the consumer or family.

Purpose

To make sure consumers with a visual, hearing or speech impairment are not denied effective notice of benefits, waivers of rights or consent to services.

Procedure

  1. At first contact, intake staff discuss the consumer's communication limits and needs with the consumer and the referring agency or person, so arrangements are ready before services start.
  2. Visual impairment: staff read aloud every document normally given to a consumer to be sure it is understood. Support services are available through the Pennsylvania Association for the Blind, (717) 766-2020.
  3. Hearing impairment: at the in-person assessment the care supervisor or designee identifies how the consumer hears best and which communication methods and aids the consumer prefers (paper and pencil, American Sign Language, Signed Exact English, lip reading, a communication board, gestures), and any hearing devices the consumer uses.
  4. If a consumer uses sign language as the primary means of communication, the agency provides a qualified interpreter at no cost for both spoken and written information. Interpreters can be arranged through ASL Services, (717) 755-3212.
  5. Family members and friends are not used as interpreters unless the consumer has been told that a qualified interpreter is available at no cost and, without any pressure, chooses a family member or friend.
  6. The consumer's choice of interpreter, and the presence of an interpreter, is documented after every visit.
  7. The consumer's service plan identifies the preferred communication method, and the care supervisor shares it with all direct care workers who serve the consumer.
  8. The consumer and family receive in writing the services that will be provided, who will provide them, their rights as they relate to the service plan, and how to reach the agency.
  9. Direct care workers may use flashcards for basic tasks such as bathing, grooming, dressing, housekeeping and meal preparation. Flashcards are not used when reviewing important documents or consent forms.

Regulations Compliance Implementation

Policy

It is the policy of the agency to:

  • Implement and enforce all state and federal regulations that apply to agency operation and management, at the main office and any branch office.
  • Comply with the regulations on consumer rights and responsibilities (see CLN.003 and AO.009).
  • Protect and promote the rights and civil liberties of each person served by the agency.

Purpose

To make sure state and federal requirements are followed and that any shortfall found is corrected through a Corrective Action Plan (CAP).

Procedure

  1. Consumer rights and responsibilities are given, reviewed and acknowledged as CLN.003 describes. Rights of the Elderly (AO.009) are also reviewed with every consumer aged 60 or older.
  2. The agency keeps a current copy of 28 Pa. Code Chapter 611 on site, as 28 Pa. Code § 611.4(a) requires, and follows the environmental, health, sanitation and professional licensure standards of federal, state and local authorities (§ 611.4(c)).
  3. The Department will monitor the agency at least once every two years. Monitoring may be announced or unannounced.
  4. The agency will submit documentation requested by the Department showing compliance with: (a) the Medical Assistance provider agreement, including the waiver addendum; (b) all Medical Assistance regulations and policies that apply to home care; (c) the approved waiver and any approved amendments; and (d) any state or federal requirement.
  5. If the Department issues a written statement of findings, the agency responds with a CAP, on the Department's form, when requested.
  6. The CAP must include: the agency's name and address; its Medical Assistance identification number; the action steps for each finding and the dates they will be completed; how the steps will correct the finding and the date the finding will be corrected; and the signature of the agency showing it will carry out the CAP.
  7. The Department reviews and monitors the CAP to make sure each finding is corrected. It may reject a CAP and ask the agency to revise it, or write a CAP for the agency to carry out. The agency carries out any CAP the Department approves.
  8. The Department may conduct a follow-up review to confirm the agency is carrying out the CAP.

Rights of the Elderly / Consumer Protection

Policy

Each consumer is informed of, and given written notice of, all policies governing consumer rights, responsibilities and conduct (see CLN.003). The agency follows the consumer protections of 28 Pa. Code § 611.57.

Procedure

  1. The consumer may exercise his or her rights without fear of reprisal. A consumer who wishes to exercise a right notifies the care supervisor or the Administrator.
  2. The consumer's understanding of his or her rights is documented in the client record.
  3. The consumer is told of any barrier to care or service and of every change in the service plan. This is documented in the client record.
  4. The consumer has the right to have his or her property treated with consideration and respect, and with full recognition of his or her individuality and personal needs.
  5. If the consumer has been adjudged incompetent, the agency allows the person appointed by law to act for the consumer to exercise the consumer's rights.
  6. If the consumer has not been adjudged incompetent, a legal representative may exercise the consumer's rights to the extent the law permits.
  7. The consumer has the right to have all personal and client records kept confidential, and the agency keeps them so.
  8. The consumer has the right to take part in planning his or her care and in planning changes to it.
  9. The consumer is given all required information on how to voice complaints and grievances, and is told that this can be done without fear of reprisal (see QAPI.003).
  10. The consumer has the right to be treated with respect.
  11. The consumer has the right to refuse services and to have the consequences explained.

Home Care Consumer Protections (28 Pa. Code § 611.57)

  1. Consumer rights. A consumer of home care services provided by a home care agency has the right: (a) to be involved in the service planning process and to receive services with reasonable accommodation of individual needs and preferences, except where the health and safety of the direct care worker is at risk; and (b) to receive at least 10 calendar days' advance written notice of the agency's intent to end services. Less than 10 days' notice may be given if the consumer has failed to pay for services despite notice and is more than 14 days in arrears, or if the health and welfare of the direct care worker is at risk (see CLN.006).
  2. Prohibitions. No individual, because of his or her affiliation with the agency, may assume power of attorney or guardianship over a consumer who uses the agency's services. The agency may not require a consumer to endorse checks over to the agency.
  3. Information packet. Before services begin, the agency gives the consumer, the consumer's legal representative or a responsible family member an information packet, in a form that is easy to read and understand, that contains:
    A.A list of the home care services the direct care worker will provide, and the name of the direct care worker who will provide them.
    B.The hours when those services will be provided.
    C.The fees and total costs for those services on an hourly or weekly basis.
    D.Whom to contact at the Department of Health for information about licensure requirements for a home care agency and for compliance information about a particular agency.
    E.The Department's complaint hotline, 1-800-254-5164, and the telephone number of the Ombudsman Program of the local Area Agency on Aging, Franklin County Area Agency on Aging, 717-263-2153 or 1-800-642-6990.
    F.The hiring and competency requirements that apply to direct care workers employed by the agency (see HR.001 and HR.004).
    G.The disclosure published by the Department in the Pennsylvania Bulletin about the employee or independent contractor status of the direct care worker providing services, and the resulting tax and insurance obligations and other responsibilities of the consumer and the agency.
  4. Documentation. The agency keeps on file documentation showing that it complies with this policy. It is available for Department inspection.

Fraudulent and Abusive Acts

Policy

The Administrator makes sure that all employees and contractors are told what acts and circumstances are fraud and abuse, and that compliance with all related federal, state and local laws, regulations and rules is monitored. Acts of fraud or abuse are not tolerated and are reported to the proper authorities.

Purpose

To prevent, find and report fraud and abuse in the agency's billing and business practices.

Procedure

  1. The agency does not charge a consumer for any item or service already paid for by Medical Assistance, a managed care plan or another payer, and returns to the consumer or representative any money collected in error.
  2. The Administrator monitors all costs and charges for supplies and services provided by outside vendors.
  3. The agency does not accept free services from a vendor in exchange for a referral by the agency or its employees (see AO.011).
  4. The Administrator makes sure that:
    A.the agency applies the "prudent buyer" concept when it contracts for outside services;
    B.the agency checks the licenses of professionals who provide services for it;
    C.every bill submitted is for services actually delivered to the consumer;
    D.processes are in place to confirm that services provided are appropriate; and
    E.documentation is available to support financial review.
  5. Professional practice acts. Services are provided in line with recognized standards and the laws that govern each discipline. Staff duties stay within the limits of the applicable state practice acts.
  6. Agency licensure and enrollment. The agency keeps its Pennsylvania license as a home care agency under 28 Pa. Code Chapter 611, keeps its Medical Assistance and managed care provider agreements current, and meets the requirements that come with them.
  7. Law enforcement requests. The agency cooperates with law enforcement requests, and responds to subpoenas for records. No records are released without proper authorization. If proper authorization cannot be determined, the Administrator may seek legal advice first. The Administrator is told immediately when any subpoena arrives.
  8. An employee who is contacted about a legal matter that might involve the agency, directly or indirectly, such as by subpoena or from an attorney, tells his or her supervisor and the Administrator before taking any action.
  9. An employee who suspects fraud or abuse reports it to the Administrator. The report may also be made to the Pennsylvania Department of Human Services Fraud Tip Line, 1-844-DHS-TIPS (1-844-347-8477). For suspected provider fraud, the report may be made to the Medical Assistance Provider Compliance Hotline, 1-866-379-8477.

Illegal Solicitation and Remuneration

Policy

Individuals employed by or under contract with the agency shall not willfully or knowingly solicit, offer, receive or pay any remuneration, including bribes, rebates or kickbacks, directly or indirectly, openly or secretly, in cash or in kind, to induce the referral of consumers whose care is paid for under any federal or state health care program.

Purpose

To prevent illegal payments for referrals.

Procedure

  1. All employees and contractors are told of this policy at orientation.
  2. Employees and contractors sign, with title and date, to show they have been instructed on the policy.
  3. Violation of this policy is grounds for immediate termination, civil suit, or a formal report to the appropriate authority.
  4. Federal and state health care programs include, but are not limited to, Medical Assistance (Medicaid, Title XIX), including home and community-based waiver programs, and other programs named in 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b.
  5. Advertising is not prohibited, except advertising that is false, misleading or deceptive.
  6. Payment for advertising, marketing or other services is not prohibited if it is set in advance, is consistent with the fair market value of the services, and is not based on the volume or value of referrals.
  7. This policy does not prohibit any payment or business arrangement that is not prohibited by 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b(b) or its regulations.
  8. Illegal remuneration is a felony and, on conviction, is punishable by a fine, imprisonment, or both.

Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) Compliance

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. does not discriminate against individuals on the basis of disability in admission to or access to its home care services, or in employment. This policy follows the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (ADA), a federal civil rights law that prohibits discrimination against people with disabilities in everyday activities, including services. It also follows Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, which prohibits discrimination on the basis of disability in programs and activities that receive federal financial assistance.

Purpose

To maintain a work environment and service culture in which everyone has an equal opportunity, to improve the quality and consistency of consumer care, and to reduce the risk of unlawful or improper conduct.

Procedure

  1. The agency has a designated coordinator to support its compliance with the ADA and Section 504 (see the Section 504 Coordinator named in AO.005).
  2. On request, the agency provides accommodation so that people with disabilities can take part in its services, employment and activities.
  3. Under Title III of the ADA, a service provider must make sure that consumers who are blind, deaf-blind or visually impaired have an equal opportunity to take part in and benefit from its goods and services. This may mean providing auxiliary aids and services, or taking steps to remove structural communication barriers. Auxiliary aids and services include readers, taped texts, braille materials, and getting or changing equipment.
  4. Staff do not make assumptions about a consumer's vision or about the functional effects of vision loss. Staff ask the consumer what is needed for mobility, hearing or vision so that accommodations can be made while keeping the consumer's personal control and dignity. This helps find and address small problems before they become large or life-threatening. Mobility disabilities include, for example, those of people who use wheelchairs, scooters, walkers or crutches, or no mobility device at all.
  5. See also AO.005 (Non-Discrimination) and AO.007 (Communicating with Visual, Hearing or Speech Impaired Consumers).

Employee Hiring / Competency Requirements

Policy

Every direct care worker must meet the hiring and competency requirements below before being assigned to a consumer, and a confidential personnel record is kept for every employee.

Purpose

To set the minimum hiring and competency steps and to make sure the agency keeps the records that show they were met.

Procedure

  1. At hiring or rostering of a direct care worker, the agency will: (a) hold a face-to-face interview; (b) obtain at least two satisfactory references that confirm the person's ability to provide home care services; and (c) complete a criminal background check and, if applicable, a ChildLine child abuse clearance as required by 28 Pa. Code 611.52 and 611.53 (see HR.002).
  2. Personnel records are confidential. They are kept in a locked file in the Human Resources file cabinet and kept current.
  3. Each personnel record contains: the application, interview notes and references; the signed job description; criminal history and child abuse clearance results; a current copy of CPR certification if required for the position; a copy of the driver's license and vehicle insurance if the employee drives for the agency; tuberculosis screening results (see HR.003); signed statements on safety and health, illegal remuneration, abuse and neglect reporting, infection control and confidentiality; the signed orientation form; and competency evaluations and tests.
  4. Form I-9 and medical information are kept in a separate folder. Wage records are kept in a separate confidential file.
  5. Personnel files are kept for at least five years after the employee leaves, unless a longer period is required.
  6. Competency: before a direct care worker is assigned to a consumer, the agency makes sure the worker has done one of the following: (a) holds a valid nurse's license in Pennsylvania; (b) passed a competency test developed by the agency; or (c) completed a training program developed by the agency or another entity that meets state regulation, a home health aide training program that meets 42 CFR 484.36, a nurse aide program approved by the Pennsylvania Department of Education, or a training program that meets the standards of a Medicaid waiver or other publicly funded home and community-based program the agency takes part in.
  7. The agency keeps documentation in the worker's file that it has reviewed the worker's competency to perform assigned duties through direct observation, testing, training, consumer feedback or another method approved by the Department, or a combination of methods. The review is repeated at least once a year after initial competency is shown.
  8. Each employee reads and signs that he or she understands the policies on safety and health, confidentiality and infection control. The signed form goes in the personnel file.

Background Checks

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. checks the background of every job candidate after a conditional offer is made, and before the person begins work. The agency follows 28 Pa. Code §§ 611.51 through 611.54 and the Older Adults Protective Services Act background check rules at 6 Pa. Code §§ 15.141 through 15.149. This policy covers direct care workers, office staff and the agency's owners.

Purpose

To make sure that employees who serve consumers are qualified, and that consumers, families and employees are safe and secure. The checks confirm credentials, criminal history, employment background and other information that bears on an employment decision.

Procedure

  1. Scope. Background checks are required for all new hires, full-time, part-time and temporary, for office staff, and for the agency's owners. The agency requires contractors to meet this policy.     C.in addition, for an applicant who has not been a Pennsylvania resident for the 2 years before the request, a federal (FBI) criminal history record and a letter of determination from the Pennsylvania Department of Aging based on it (§ 611.52(c); 6 Pa. Code § 15.144(b));
    D.a ChildLine child abuse clearance, for every direct care worker and office staff applicant, if the agency serves consumers under 18 (§ 611.53); and
    E.a face-to-face interview and at least two satisfactory references (§ 611.51).

  9. Prohibitions. The agency does not hire, roster or retain a person if the State Police criminal history record shows a prohibited conviction listed in 6 Pa. The candidate is given a copy of the report and information on how to appeal to the source of the criminal history records if the person believes they are in error (§ 611.52(h)).

Education credentials beyond high school that qualify the person for the position are reviewed, and verified through official transcripts if questioned. Licenses are verified with the state licensing board and checked for sanctions such as exclusion, suspension, revocation, probation or debarment, and re-verified each year at the performance evaluation. Prior employment is verified in writing where possible (or by phone by the supervisor), with dates of employment and reason for leaving, and at least two prior employment references are kept in the file.

  14. Confidentiality. Information from State Police records and Department of Aging letters is kept confidential and is used only to decide whether a person may be hired, rostered or retained (§ 611.52(g)).
  15. Exceptions. A direct care worker who has complied with § 611.52 and who transfers to another agency owned and operated by the same entity does not need another criminal history report. A direct care worker employed by an entity that changes ownership does not need another report for the new owner (§ 611.52(i)).
  16. Personal documents. A new employee brings copies of diplomas and licenses on the first day. They are verified and copies are kept in the personnel file.

Employee Health Screening

Policy

Every direct care worker who will have contact with consumers must be screened for tuberculosis (TB) before that contact, and the screening must be updated every 12 months, as required by 28 Pa. Code 611.56.

Purpose

To protect consumers and workers from the spread of TB and to keep a record of each worker's screening.

Procedure

  1. Screening follows CDC guidelines. Before first contact with a consumer, each worker has a TB test (a two-step skin test, or a blood test) or provides proof of a negative test within the past year.
  2. Known positive reactors provide a copy of a previous chest x-ray if available and complete a TB symptom questionnaire every year. A chest x-ray with follow-up is required if the worker has symptoms.
  3. Skin tests must be read by a licensed health care provider 48 to 72 hours after they are given, or they are not valid and must be repeated. The provider signs the form, and the signed form goes in the employee's confidential health file.
  4. Workers are told of any abnormal result. A positive result requires follow-up and treatment as the health care provider directs.
  5. A worker who tests positive is not scheduled for consumer contact until the worker's health care provider gives a written release to work.
  6. Each worker provides updated screening documentation at least every 12 months, counted from the date of the last screening. The care supervisor tracks the due dates.

Emergency Preparedness / Disaster Plan

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. is committed to providing needed services to consumers during emergencies, whether caused by weather, a civil disturbance or another event. The agency's goal is to continue services with as little interruption as possible while protecting the safety of staff and consumers. The Administrator or care supervisor is told of all emergencies and is responsible for activating this plan.

Purpose

To keep consumers safe and services going during an emergency, and to meet the back-up and emergency planning requirements of the agency's waiver and payer agreements.

Procedure

  1. Staff notification: when the plan is activated, the Administrator or care supervisor starts a phone calling chain to reach all staff. Staff keep an updated list of staff phone numbers. If all agency phones fail, staff listen to the Emergency Broadcast System. Staff may be asked to take on different duties during an emergency.
  2. Consumer notification and priority: at the start of services, the care supervisor identifies consumers who would be at high risk in an emergency and reviews the emergency plan with them and their caregivers. Each consumer is assigned a priority level (see 10). If an emergency lasts more than a day, the agency tries to contact consumers daily to check on their status and needs.
  3. Continuation of services: the care supervisor keeps an up-to-date list of active consumers with name, address and phone number, checks which staff are available given where the emergency is, and reassigns workers using the priority list. If an emergency prevents services, the Administrator may ask local, county or state emergency services for help, and may arrange alternatives such as another agency. Vendors providing critical supplies are expected to have their own emergency plan.
  4. Restoration: the Administrator decides when the emergency is over. The care supervisor contacts staff and consumers to find current needs. Handwritten documentation is turned in to the office within forty-eight (48) hours of the return to normal operations.
  5. Office emergencies: for fire, bomb threat or gas leak, the person who finds the threat announces the need to evacuate and calls 911. Staff leave by the posted exit plan and meet at the designated spot outside. The Administrator or designee checks that everyone is out, and staff do not go back in until told it is safe. Staff may use a fire extinguisher on a small fire. For a power failure, staff stay put and the electric company and landlord are called; if power is off for more than an hour the office closes and the on-call procedure (AO.003) is used. For a phone failure, the phone company is called and the Administrator decides whether to call consumers by cell phone or forward the office lines. Computer data is backed up each business day and a copy is kept off-site.
  6. Loss of records: duplicate computer backup files are kept securely off-site. Paper records are kept in locked cabinets. Lost or destroyed records are recreated from backups.
  7. Employee emergencies: if a worker is unable to continue a visit, the care supervisor decides which consumers still need service and reassigns them using the priority list. Rescheduled visits are documented. For a temporary staffing shortage, the Administrator or care supervisor contacts other staff, assigns management staff to visit if appropriate, and prioritizes visits. For a critical shortage, the consumer or family is told, offered the choice of moving to another agency, and the referral source and service coordinator are told.
  8. Weather and community-wide emergencies: the Administrator makes all decisions about operations. The weather service and local emergency management alerts are monitored. The calling chain is used, visits are prioritized, consumers are told of changes, and families are shown what to do in advance when possible. Canceled visits are documented and the service coordinator is told.
  9. Communication and training: the Administrator is the only spokesperson with the press and distributes a new emergency contact plan every year. The plan is covered in new staff orientation and reviewed with all staff every year. Building evacuation drills are held at least once a year, and fire safety training including hands-on use of a fire extinguisher is held at least every two (2) years.
  10. Consumer priority listing: the care supervisor assigns every consumer a level. High (first priority): the consumer lives alone with no family or caregiver support, would be harmed if care were missed, or needs daily or timed services. These consumers are visited if it is safe. Moderate: no significant harm is expected if services are postponed one or two days. A capable caregiver may be coached by phone, and the consumer is called and visited if possible. Low: services can be postponed without harm, and the consumer is called. If the emergency makes it unsafe for staff, visits are not made.

Universal / Standard Precautions

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. reduces the risk of exposure to infection, and prevents the spread of infection to consumers and workers, by using universal (standard) precautions with every consumer.

Purpose

To help prevent and control the spread of disease that is passed on through exposure to blood and other potentially infectious materials, as the OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard (29 CFR 1910.1030) requires.

Overview

  • Direct care workers use universal precautions with every consumer. When in contact with a consumer's blood or other potentially infectious material, workers treat all body fluids as potentially infectious.
  • Workers who give direct care have personal protective equipment (PPE) available to carry out universal precautions. It is available through the agency office (see SIC.006).
  • Every worker is responsible for knowing how and where to get PPE and how to store and dispose of it. This is covered at orientation.
  • At orientation and every year, all workers receive education on universal precautions.

Procedure

  1. Workers are given paper towels and antimicrobial hand soap to use in the consumer's home.
  2. Hands are always washed before and after consumer contact, after any contact with body fluids, and after removing gloves.
  3. When running water is not available, workers use the hand cleanser the agency provides and wash their hands with soap and water as soon as possible after the visit.
  4. Gloves are worn whenever there is contact, or potential contact, with blood, non-intact skin, mucous membranes or other potentially infectious material.
  5. Gloves are also worn when handling any soiled item, including: dressings or bandages; contaminated clothing and linens; items used to care for abrasions, cuts, sores or other broken or chafed skin; materials used to clean up spills of body fluids, urine or blood; and when giving personal hygiene care. Workers do not handle needles or other sharps; if one is found in the home, the worker does not touch it and tells the supervisor.
  6. Gloves are replaced if cracked, peeling, torn or punctured.
  7. Masks, face shields or protective eyewear are worn whenever splashing of blood or other body fluids is possible.
  8. A protective apron is worn whenever clothing could be contaminated with blood or other body fluids. Aprons are made of fluid-resistant material.
  9. Workers who are trained in CPR carry a resuscitation mask for use if a consumer needs CPR.
  10. A worker who has an accident or injury from a needle stick or sharp, or a splash of blood or other potentially infectious material into the eyes, nose or mouth, reports it to the Administrator or care supervisor immediately (see SIC.004).

Exposure Control Plan

Policy

The agency keeps a program to make sure that all exposures to blood or body substances are reported promptly and managed according to the guidelines of the Centers for Disease Control (CDC) and the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA).

Purpose

To protect employees from blood-borne pathogens and to meet the OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard.

Procedure

  1. Exposure determination: direct care workers who provide bathing and personal care are expected to have exposure (Class I). Office staff are not expected to have exposure (Class III). The Administrator reviews these classes whenever job duties change.
  2. All staff use standard infection control and universal precautions. Engineering and work practice controls to reduce exposure include: leak-proof containers for contaminated items and protective equipment; hand washing before and after consumer care and before and after wearing gloves, with liquid soap, alcohol-based sanitizer and paper towels provided; contaminated sharps are never bent, recapped or broken and are disposed of in a sharps container; and equipment is cleaned after each use with germicide, bleach or alcohol.
  3. Personal protective equipment is provided at no cost to the employee and chosen for the exposure expected. It must not let blood or other infectious material reach the employee's clothing, skin, eyes, mouth or mucous membranes. Workers carry the supply they need. Contaminated equipment is removed as soon as possible and placed in a plastic bag in a garbage receptacle. Clean equipment is removed and discarded before leaving the consumer's home. Laundry is handled by universal precautions.
  4. Hepatitis B vaccine: employees who could be exposed to blood are offered the vaccine within 10 days of starting work. Employees who already have the vaccine or decline it sign a waiver, and may ask for the vaccine later. The Administrator or care supervisor makes sure the vaccine is offered and the waivers are signed.
  5. After an exposure: the employee reports it to the Administrator or care supervisor immediately and completes the exposure incident form. The employee is sent promptly to a licensed health care provider for evaluation. When indicated, the consumer or family is asked for consent to test the source blood and to release HIV-related information. If the consumer refuses, the refusal is documented in writing. The evaluating provider certifies the exposure, orders testing and decides what follow-up is needed. The provider's written report goes in the employee's confidential medical file. The agency pays for source and employee testing and consultation fees.
  6. Training: all staff are trained at orientation and every year on the OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard, how blood-borne diseases spread, and this plan.
  7. Employee medical records are kept separately from personnel files by the Administrator, for the duration of employment plus the period OSHA requires. Each record holds the employee's name, hepatitis B vaccine consent or declination and date of vaccination, and, after an exposure, the exposure report, consent forms, evaluation, test results and follow-up. Only the Administrator, approved supervisory staff and the affected employee have access.

Disposal of Contaminated Materials

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. reduces the risk of exposure to infection by disposing of potentially contaminated items properly.

Purpose

To keep workers, consumers and the public from exposure to blood and body fluids in soiled items.

Procedure

  1. Items soiled with blood or body fluids, such as dressings, incontinence products, sanitary napkins, paper towels and used PPE, are discarded as follows:

    B.Remove contaminated materials from the consumer's home as soon as possible, and place them in a tamper-resistant trash receptacle.
    C.Wash hands before and after handling the bag.

  3. Laundry:
    A.Contaminated laundry is separated and placed in a secured plastic bag, and is washed separately.
    B.Dry linens with no sign of contamination are placed with the consumer's regular laundry.
  4. Sharps: workers do not pick up, bend, recap or break needles or other sharps. If sharps are found in the home, the worker tells the supervisor, who tells the consumer how to dispose of them safely in a puncture-resistant container.
  5. The consumer and caregiver are instructed in the correct way to discard contaminated materials and body fluids.

Personal Protective Equipment

Policy

Personal protective equipment (PPE) is provided, used and maintained whenever it has been determined that its use is required to ensure the safety and health of employees and will lessen the likelihood of work-related injury or illness. This policy follows OSHA's PPE standards at 29 CFR 1910.132 and following.

Purpose

To protect the employees of Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. from workplace hazards and the risk of injury through PPE. PPE is not a substitute for more effective controls. It is used only when other means of protection against hazards and hazardous chemicals are not adequate or practical, and together with other controls unless no other means of hazard control exists. This policy covers general PPE requirements, including eye and face, head, foot and leg, hand and arm, and body protection, and covers: responsibilities of supervisors and employees; hazard assessment and PPE selection; employee training; and cleaning and maintenance of PPE.

Responsibilities

  1. The Administrator develops, carries out and administers the PPE policies. This includes:
    A.seeing that workplace hazard assessments are done to find hazards that require PPE;
    B.selecting and purchasing PPE;
    C.reviewing, updating and doing PPE hazard assessments whenever a job changes, new equipment is used, there has been an accident, a supervisor or employee asks for one, and at least every year;
    D.keeping records of PPE assignments, training and hazard assessments;
    E.providing training, guidance and help to supervisors and employees on the use, care and cleaning of approved PPE;
    F.re-evaluating the suitability of selected PPE from time to time; and
    G.reviewing and evaluating the effectiveness of PPE use, training and policies.
  2. Supervisors have the main responsibility for carrying out and enforcing PPE use and policy. This includes:
    A.providing appropriate PPE and making it available to employees;
    B.making sure employees are trained in the use, care and cleaning of PPE, and that competency forms are completed, signed and given to the Administrator;
    C.making sure employees use and maintain PPE properly and follow this policy;
    D.telling the Administrator when new hazards, processes or changes are introduced; and
    E.making sure damaged or defective PPE is discarded and replaced at once.
  3. Employees follow the PPE policies. This includes wearing PPE as required, attending training, caring for, cleaning, maintaining and inspecting PPE as required, and telling the supervisor when PPE needs repair or replacement.

Procedure

  1. Hazard assessment. The Administrator makes a walk-through survey of both home and office environments to identify sources of hazards. The work area surveyed, the person doing the survey, the hazards found and the date are documented, and the Administrator keeps the forms. The assessment is repeated whenever a job changes, new equipment or process is installed, there has been an accident, a supervisor or employee asks, and at least every year. Any new PPE requirement is added to this program.
  2. Selection of PPE. When hazards have been identified, the Administrator first decides whether they can be eliminated or reduced by means that do not rely on employee behavior, such as engineering controls. If those are not adequate or practical, the Administrator selects PPE that gives a level of protection greater than the minimum required, taking account of multiple, simultaneous hazards. All PPE is of safe design and construction for the work and is kept in a sanitary and reliable condition. Only items that meet NIOSH or ANSI standards are bought or accepted for use. Employees whose jobs require PPE are told of the selection and are given PPE by the agency at no charge, in a size and fit that will be used.
  3. Training. A worker required to wear PPE is trained in its use and care before doing work that needs it, and is retrained as needed. Training covers: when PPE is necessary; what PPE is necessary; how to put on, take off, adjust and wear it; its limits; and its care, maintenance, useful life and disposal. After training, the employee shows that he or she can use PPE correctly, or is retrained. Each employee's training is documented and kept on file.
  4. Retraining. Retraining is needed when an employee's work habits or knowledge show a lack of understanding or skill, when new equipment is installed, when changes in the workplace make earlier training out of date, or when the types of PPE change.
  5. Cleaning and maintenance. PPE is kept clean and in good condition. Employees inspect, clean and maintain PPE according to the manufacturer's instructions before and after each use. PPE is not shared between employees until it has been properly cleaned and sanitized; it is issued for individual use whenever possible. If employees provide their own PPE, the Administrator makes sure it is adequate for the hazards and kept clean and reliable. Defective or damaged PPE is discarded and replaced. Contaminated PPE that cannot be decontaminated is disposed of in a way that protects employees (see SIC.005).
  6. PPE in use at the agency. Office staff: rubber gloves for toner and cleaning products; goggles for splash from cleaning products; face mask for fumes from cleaning products or toner. Direct care workers: gloves for chemicals and body fluids; goggles for chemical splash; face masks in case of inhalation hazards; aprons or gowns for splash of chemicals or body fluids.

Hazard Communication (Right to Know)

Policy

In line with the OSHA Hazard Communication Standard (29 CFR 1910.1200), Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. gives employees information about the physical and health hazards of the chemicals they may meet on the job, and protects them from uncontrolled exposure. Employees have a legal right to know about the hazardous chemicals they work with.

Purpose

To make sure employees who may come in contact with hazardous chemicals know the hazards, read the labels and Safety Data Sheets (SDS), and know how to protect themselves.

Definitions

  1. Hazardous chemical: any chemical that is a physical hazard or a health hazard.
  2. Physical hazard: a chemical that is, for example, flammable, explosive, an oxidizer, a compressed gas, corrosive to metal, or reactive.
  3. Health hazard: a chemical that can cause acute or chronic health effects in exposed employees, such as a carcinogen, toxic agent, irritant, corrosive, sensitizer, or agent that harms the lungs, skin, eyes or mucous membranes.
  4. Label: written, printed or graphic information on or attached to a container of a hazardous chemical.
  5. Safety Data Sheet (SDS): the written document, in the 16-section format of 29 CFR 1910.1200(g), that the manufacturer or importer supplies for each hazardous chemical. It replaces the older Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS).
  6. Exposure: when an employee, in the course of work, is subjected to a hazardous chemical through inhalation, ingestion, skin contact or absorption, including potential exposure.
  7. Workplace: the agency office, and any consumer's home or community location where employees work.

Procedure

  1. Chemical list. The Administrator keeps a list of the hazardous chemicals the agency supplies to employees for cleaning and office use, using the Chemical Inventory and Safety Data Sheet Log that follows this policy. The list is reviewed at least once a year and whenever a new product is added.
  2. Safety Data Sheets. The agency obtains and keeps a current SDS for each hazardous chemical on the list, and keeps them where employees can read them during each work shift. Employees may ask the Administrator for any SDS.
  3. Labels. Containers of hazardous chemicals are not used unless the manufacturer's label is on them. If a chemical is moved to another container, the new container is labeled with the product name and the hazards.
  4. Training. Before an employee first works with a hazardous chemical, and whenever a new hazard is introduced, the Administrator or designee trains the employee on: how to read labels and SDS; the hazards of the chemicals; how to protect against them, including the PPE to use (see SIC.006); and what to do after an exposure.
  5. Consumer's home. Workers use only the cleaning products that the agency provides or that the consumer has chosen for the household, follow label directions, never mix chemicals, and tell the supervisor of any chemical they find unsafe.
  6. Record keeping. The Administrator or designee keeps the chemical list, the SDS and training records. Training is documented on the agency's In-Service or Training Documentation Form, and a copy goes in each employee's file. Training records are kept for at least three (3) years and show the employee's name, date of training, an outline of the material, and the signatures of trainer and employee. Exposure records are kept as OSHA requires (29 CFR 1910.1020).

Release of Client Information

Policy

The client record is kept for the benefit of the consumer and the agency and is the property of Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. The consumer has a legal right to the information in the record, subject to legal limits such as those for minors or for people found legally incompetent. Information is released for any purpose other than the consumer's services only with the express written authorization of the consumer or the consumer's legal representative, or as the law requires.

Purpose

To protect the confidentiality of consumer information and to follow HIPAA and Pennsylvania law on release of records.

Procedure

  1. All information in a client record is confidential. Records of current consumers are not reviewed or copied by anyone not connected with the consumer's services unless the consumer gives permission.

An authorization to release information must be in writing and contain: the agency's name and address; the person or organization the information goes to; the consumer's name; the purpose and the specific information to be released; the time period covered; and the signature and date. (An Authorization for Use or Disclosure of Protected Health Information form is attached.)
  5. Faxed information is preceded by an agency cover sheet showing the requester's name, fax and telephone number. A copy of the fax is filed in the client record. Information is faxed only to providers or agencies involved in the consumer's services.
  6. HIV-related information: this information may be released only with a specific written HIV disclosure authorization from the consumer, to the consumer after a signed disclosure authorization, under a valid court order (a subpoena alone is not enough), or as the Pennsylvania Confidentiality of HIV-Related Information Act otherwise allows.
  7. A consumer's information may also be shared without authorization with the state or a payer as required to oversee, monitor or pay for services, and as required by law, such as to report suspected abuse or neglect.
  8. Requests from attorneys, courts or law enforcement are sent to the Administrator before anything is released.
