2.0 Company Operations

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. is a licensed home care agency in Pennsylvania, operating under 28 Pa. Code Chapter 611. We provide non-medical home care services that help consumers stay safe, comfortable and as independent as possible in their own homes, while respecting the role of the family. Our direct care workers help with personal care and activities of daily living such as bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, mobility, meal preparation, light housekeeping, errands and companionship, in accordance with each consumer’s service plan. We do not provide skilled medical or nursing care.

The success of the Agency depends on providing the best possible care to our consumers every day.

2.1 Company Expectations

The Agency expects every employee to: show a positive attitude toward consumer care; treat every consumer, family member and co-worker with respect and dignity; work as part of a team; complete all required documentation promptly and accurately; create a supportive culture where honesty and fairness prevail; strictly uphold the policies of the Agency and comply with state and federal regulations; and act professionally at all times, whether in the office, in the community or in a consumer’s home.

3.0 Equal Opportunity Statement

The Agency is an equal employment opportunity employer and does not discriminate against employees or job applicants on the basis of race, religion, color, sex, pregnancy, age, national origin, mental or physical disability, veteran or family status, genetic information, or any other status or condition protected by applicable federal, state or local laws.

This policy extends to all aspects of the employment relationship, including recruiting, interviewing, job assignments, training, compensation, discipline, termination, and all other terms, conditions and privileges of employment.

3.1 Immigration Law Compliance

In accordance with the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 (IRCA), the Agency employs only individuals who are legally authorized to work in the United States, and does not continue to employ anyone whose legal right to work has ended.

U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services Form I-9 is used to verify your identity and employment eligibility. You must complete the employee section of Form I-9 and provide the required documents before you may begin working.

3.2 Americans with Disabilities Act Compliance

The Agency adheres to the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), as amended, and makes every effort to ensure that qualified individuals with a disability are not discriminated against in any terms, conditions or privileges of employment. The ADA requires employers to provide reasonable accommodation to qualified individuals with known disabilities in all aspects of employment, unless the accommodation would cause an undue hardship to the employer.

An individual with a disability is a person who: (1) has a physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one or more major life activities; (2) has a record of such an impairment; or (3) is regarded as having such an impairment (in which case no reasonable accommodation is required).

A qualified individual is a person with a disability who meets the skill, education, experience, training and other job-related requirements of the position and who, with or without reasonable accommodation, can perform its essential functions. The Agency is committed to providing reasonable accommodation to the known physical or mental limitations of such individuals so they can perform the essential functions of a job, unless doing so would create an undue hardship.

If you need an accommodation under the ADA, notify the Administrator immediately.

4.1 Employment Classifications

As an employee of the Agency, you are an “employee at will.” This means either you or the Agency may end the employment relationship at any time, with or without cause and with or without advance notice. We request, as a courtesy, that you give two weeks’ advance notice of your intention to quit.

Nothing in this Manual or any other Agency document, other than a written employment contract signed by the parties, modifies the at-will policy or means that termination will occur only for “just cause.” This Manual does not create an express or implied contract of employment for any definite period. Any statement, written or oral, that conflicts with the at-will policy is void.

Regular Full-Time is an employee who has no termination date and is regularly scheduled to work forty (40) or more hours per week.

Regular Part-Time is an employee whose position has no termination date and who is regularly scheduled to work fewer than forty (40) hours per week.

Temporary Employee is an employee hired for a certain length of time and paid only for hours worked.

Provisional Employee is an employee who has not yet completed the ninety (90) day provisional period after being hired, as described in Section 4.16. At-will employment remains at-will after the provisional period.

Exempt Employee is generally an executive, professional, administrator or manager who is paid a salary without overtime. Nonexempt Employee is an employee who does not qualify for exempt status and is paid on an hourly basis, including overtime.

Any concern about your classification should be addressed to your supervisor.

4.2 Confidentiality

As a result of your employment, you will have access to confidential information belonging to the Agency and its consumers. This includes personnel information, procedures, financial information, records, business plans, consumer names, lists and contracts, and any other information specific to the Agency. You will also learn private health, personal and financial information about consumers and their families, which is protected by law, including the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA).

As a condition of employment, you agree that all such information is the exclusive property of the Agency or the consumer and that you will not disclose it to anyone, except in the responsible exercise of your job, whether or not it has been marked “confidential.” Share consumer information only with people who need it to provide or supervise care. Do not discuss it in public places or on social media, do not take photographs or recordings of consumers or their records, and do not share it with friends or family. Keep paper and electronic records secure and return them to the office as instructed. Report any lost record, or any suspected unauthorized access or disclosure, to the Administrator immediately.

Signing a separate confidentiality agreement at the Agency’s request is also a condition of continued employment, and in the event of conflict that agreement controls. Your duty of confidentiality continues after you leave the Agency. If you are unsure of your obligations, consult your supervisor.

4.3 Personal Information

It is important that the Agency’s personnel records be accurate at all times. To avoid problems with tax liability, pay, or our ability to contact you about shift changes, promptly notify your supervisor of any change in your name, home address, telephone number, number of dependents, emergency contact, or any other information pertinent to your employment. You must submit a new IRS Form W-4 any time the information on it changes.

4.4 Attendance and Punctuality

The Agency believes that a good record of attendance and punctuality is an essential component of good work performance. Consumers depend on you. You are expected to be at the consumer’s home, dressed appropriately and ready to work, by your scheduled start time.

If you are unable to report on time, or unable to remain until the end of your shift, you must notify your supervisor or the office directly, as early as possible and at least two (2) hours before your start time, so that a replacement can be arranged. Never leave a consumer alone before relief arrives or the consumer’s service plan permits.

All time off must be requested in advance, in writing. Excessive absences may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

All notifications of absence must be made face to face, in writing, or by telephone call to your supervisor or the office, as designated from time to time. You may not call in sick by email or text message alone.

4.5 Dress Code

As an employee of the Agency, you must maintain a clean, neat appearance. Your attire should be consistent with the type of work you are performing and with safety considerations: clean clothing, closed-toe non-slip shoes, short clean fingernails and good personal hygiene. Your Agency photo ID badge must be worn and visible on every visit. Any required uniform or safety equipment will be provided.

Good personal grooming and hygiene are essential and contribute to a professional appearance. If you have questions about expected attire, discuss them with your supervisor.

4.6 Work Hours and Overtime Pay

Nonexempt (Hourly) Employees: The work week begins at 12:01 AM Monday and ends at midnight the following Sunday. The Agency does not guarantee that you will work any particular number of hours in a given day or week, because hours depend on consumer service plans. Overtime is worked only when approved in advance by your supervisor. You are expected to work necessary overtime when requested, and you will receive time and one-half your regular rate for hours actually worked over forty (40) in a work week. Time off from work, such as holidays or jury duty, is not counted as hours worked even if paid.

Exempt (Salaried) Employees: The same work week applies. Exempt employees are not paid overtime; some work over forty (40) hours per week is built into the salary. If you are overburdened and cannot complete your assignments with a moderate amount of additional work, speak to your supervisor.

4.7 Time Clock, Time Cards and Visit Records

Record your start and end time for every visit accurately, using the Agency’s electronic visit verification (EVV) system or the timesheet your supervisor provides. You may not punch or sign in for another employee. If a punch is missed or incorrect, tell your supervisor right away; the supervisor will note the correct time and initial the correction, and must approve every record that has been adjusted. Failure to clock in and out may result in loss of pay for unverifiable work and, if repeated, disciplinary action up to and including termination.

Time cards and time sheets must be completed accurately and on time. Your signature, and the consumer’s where required, certifies that the record shows the time actually worked. Falsifying a time record is a fraudulent act for which an employee may be disciplined up to and including termination and may be prosecuted.

4.8 Meal Periods

Nonexempt employees are allowed a thirty (30) minute unpaid meal break when working a long shift, taken at a time that does not interfere with the consumer’s care. Any other breaks must be approved in advance by your supervisor and are unpaid. If a consumer’s care does not allow an uninterrupted meal break, tell your supervisor so that the time can be paid. Exempt employees may take a meal break and a reasonable number of other breaks at their discretion.

4.9 Safety and Accident Rules

Safety is a priority at the Agency. We strive to provide a safe environment in accordance with the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970. You are expected to take part in maintaining it: observe all posted safety rules, follow your supervisor’s safety instructions, use required safety equipment, and learn the location of safety and emergency equipment and the emergency telephone numbers. Report any problem with safety equipment to your supervisor. If it is not safe to work for any reason, report the problem to your supervisor immediately. You may leave a home where you are threatened or feel unsafe and call your supervisor.

Use safe lifting and transfer methods, and ask for help rather than lifting beyond your ability. Report every incident, injury, fall, complaint, consumer emergency or unsafe condition to your supervisor immediately and complete an incident report within 24 hours. In an emergency call 911 first.

All work-related accidents are covered by workers’ compensation insurance under Pennsylvania law. Report every work injury or illness to your supervisor immediately, even if minor. Failure to promptly report an accident, injury or illness may result in loss of coverage.

Wash your hands before and after each consumer, wear gloves when handling blood or body fluids, and follow standard precautions. Under OSHA’s Bloodborne Pathogens standard, the Agency offers the Hepatitis B vaccination series at no cost to employees with occupational exposure; if you decline, you must sign a declination and may change your mind at any time. Report any needle stick or exposure to blood or body fluids immediately. Do not report to work, and call your supervisor, if you have a fever, vomiting, diarrhea or another contagious illness.

4.10 Smoking

Our goal is to provide a healthy and pleasant environment. The Agency prohibits any form of tobacco or vape use on Agency premises, and you may not smoke or vape in a consumer’s home while working.

4.11 Use of Company Property

The Agency will provide the equipment necessary for you to do your job. None of this equipment may be used for personal purposes, and no equipment may be removed from Agency premises unless approved by your supervisor. This includes Agency telephones, tablets, tools and any other equipment. Any items or packages taken out of the workplace, and any desk, file cabinet or storage space provided to you, are subject to inspection at any time.

Personal telephone calls, text messages and Internet use are not to be made on Agency phones or on Agency time unless authorized by your supervisor. Use of Agency stationery, supplies or postage for personal use is prohibited. Agency premises, telephones and email may not be used to solicit collections or donations, sell raffles, goods or services, or operate betting pools. You may not use or take a consumer’s property, vehicle, money, cards or belongings for any personal purpose.

4.12 Use of Company Computers, E-mail and Internet

Use of Agency computers, printers and electronic equipment is for job-related or approved activities only. Inappropriate use may subject you to discipline, up to and including termination. Inappropriate use includes, but is not limited to: sending or receiving messages, pictures or files that are illegal, pornographic, sexist, racist, harassing or discriminatory (if you receive such material, notify your supervisor immediately); loading software not approved by management; making illegal copies of licensed software; using software or techniques that give unauthorized access to Agency computers or disrupt equipment; using Agency computers or email for personal or non-Agency use, including shopping, blogging and social media, unless authorized; and sending or posting Agency or consumer confidential information by email, text, instant message, or posting to any web site, blog or social media site.

Employees may be disciplined or terminated for inappropriate use of the Internet, email, text messages, blog posts or social networking sites even where the use does not involve Agency computers or property. Do not assume such communications are private; they may come to the Agency’s attention and result in discipline.

Any message or file created or sent using an Agency computer or electronic device is the property of the Agency. You should have no expectation of privacy in any message or file created, stored or sent on Agency equipment, and the Agency reserves the right to review, monitor, access, audit, copy, retrieve and delete any work you do on an Agency computer, including email. If provided, your Agency email account and Internet access are for business only. You are not authorized to access the computers, email accounts or files of any other employee except as authorized by your supervisor.

You must take reasonable precautions against receiving or spreading computer viruses and against wasting computer resources.

4.13 Use of Mobile Communication Devices

Employee use of Agency cellular telephones, tablets and other mobile devices is for job-related or approved activities only. Use of your personal mobile device during work hours or on Agency premises is also subject to restriction. These policies apply to any device that makes or receives calls, emails, text messages or photographs, or can browse the Internet.

Personal devices are a distraction while working and can affect consumer safety. Make personal calls during approved breaks and meal periods. While providing care, personal phones must be silenced and not used for calls, texts, photographs or social media.

Exceptions: This policy does not apply to phones supplied by the Agency and used exclusively for Agency business, or to an emergency that requires you to be reachable by phone. You may also use a personal phone for Agency business such as EVV, as instructed. If you are in doubt about what is an emergency, consult your supervisor.

4.14 Substance Abuse Policy

The Agency takes seriously the problem of drug and alcohol abuse and is committed to providing a workplace free of such substances. This policy applies to all employees.

No employee may consume, possess, sell or purchase alcoholic beverages on any property owned, leased or operated by the Agency, in a consumer’s home while working, or in any Agency vehicle. No employee may use, possess, sell, transfer or purchase any drug or controlled substance that may alter mental or physical capacity while working. The exceptions are over-the-counter medicines used as directed and drugs prescribed to you and used as your doctor prescribed. If a prescription may affect your ability to work safely, tell your supervisor. The Agency will not tolerate employees who are impaired by alcohol or drugs while working.

Where the use of alcohol or drugs poses a threat to the safety of others or property, you must report it. Employees who violate this policy will be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

Within the limits of applicable law, the Agency reserves the right to test for drugs and alcohol, including: in conjunction with an offer of employment; where there are reasonable grounds to believe an employee is under the influence; as part of an investigation of a workplace accident where there are reasonable grounds to suspect drugs or alcohol contributed; on a random basis where allowed by statute; as a follow-up to a rehabilitation program where allowed by statute; and as necessary for the safety of employees, consumers or the public where allowed by statute. Tested employees may receive a copy of the laboratory results. Compliance with this policy is a condition of employment and continued employment.

4.15 Harassment and Discrimination Policy

The Agency is proud of its work environment in which all employees are treated with respect and dignity. All employees have the right to work in an environment free from illegal discrimination or harassment, including racial and sexual harassment. Any form of discrimination or harassment, whether verbal or physical, whether in the workplace, in a consumer’s home, at work assignments off-site, at Agency functions or elsewhere, and whether by a co-worker, supervisor, consumer or family member, is unacceptable and will not be tolerated. This policy prohibits harassment and discrimination on the basis of race, religion, color, sex, age, national origin, mental or physical disability, veteran or family status, or any other status protected by law. We must all be sensitive to and tolerant of the backgrounds of others. When in doubt, don’t say it or do it.

Sexual Harassment is unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal, non-verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature when (1) submission to or rejection of the conduct is used explicitly or implicitly as a factor in decisions affecting hiring, evaluation, promotion or other aspects of employment, or (2) the conduct substantially interferes with an individual’s employment or creates an intimidating, hostile or offensive work environment. Examples include unwanted advances; demands for sexual favors in exchange for favorable treatment or continued employment; repeated sexual jokes, flirtations or propositions; verbal abuse of a sexual nature; graphic commentary about a person’s body; leering; whistling; touching; pinching; assault; coerced sexual acts; obscene comments, gestures, emails and texts; and display of sexually suggestive objects or pictures.

Racial Harassment is all inappropriate conduct taken against an individual because of race or national origin. Examples include racial comments, racial jokes or emails, treating a person differently because of race, and all other activities prohibited by Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

If you believe you have been the victim of harassment or discrimination, you should: (A) report and discuss the matter with your supervisor; and (B) if you believe your supervisor or manager is the source of or a participant in the harassment, report it to another supervisor or the Administrator.

The Agency will investigate and attempt to resolve your complaint, and take any warranted disciplinary action, as soon as possible. If for any reason you believe this has not occurred within a reasonable period, refer the problem to the Administrator. Retaliation against any individual who makes a good-faith complaint, or who cooperates in an investigation, is strictly prohibited and should be reported immediately.

4.16 Performance and Salary Reviews; Provisional Period

Each new, promoted or transferred employee will be given an oral or written job description that details the requirements and expectations of the position. Performance reviews will normally be conducted every six (6) months from the date of hire, with the exception of the three (3) month review at the end of the provisional period. Your competency is also reviewed at least once a year as required by Pennsylvania regulation.

Reviews are based on the job description and work performance. Wage increases will be based on those results as well as dependability, cooperation, attitude and any disciplinary actions, in addition to overall business and financial outlook. Your supervisor will review and discuss with you your wage or salary, your position and expectations.

During the ninety (90) day provisional period after being hired, you will not be considered a regular employee until you have satisfactorily completed it, as determined by management. The purpose of the provisional period is to determine your job satisfaction and allow the Agency to evaluate your performance. During this period you may or may not be eligible for some benefits offered to regular employees. The end of the provisional period does not change your at-will status.

4.17 Payroll

You will be paid on the regularly scheduled payday for the pay period in which the work was performed. The Agency will post a notice stating the regular paydays and the time and place of payment. If a regular payday falls on a weekend or holiday, you will be paid on the next business day. Pay is issued by direct deposit or, where permitted, by check, and you will receive a pay statement.

If there is an error in your pay, notify your supervisor immediately and every effort will be made to correct it as quickly as possible. If a paycheck is lost or stolen, notify your supervisor immediately; a new check will be issued after payment is stopped on the original. The Agency is not obligated to indemnify you for any loss if payment cannot be stopped.

The Agency will deduct Social Security, income tax and all other legally required deductions from each paycheck.

4.18 Reporting Absences

All leave requests and approvals should be made in writing whenever possible. After three (3) days of unauthorized absence, and if the Agency receives no acceptable explanation, the Agency may assume that you have resigned.

5.0 Home Care Requirements

Required screening and clearances. As required by 28 Pa. Code Chapter 611, before you have contact with any consumer you must have: a Pennsylvania State Police criminal history check; a Child Abuse History Clearance; a TB screening; an interview; two references; and verification of your credentials or training. TB screening is repeated every twelve (12) months. You must tell the Administrator within 24 hours if you are charged with or convicted of a crime or if a clearance or license changes. Your competency is checked before you work alone and reviewed at least annually, and you must complete required annual training.

Consumer rights. Every consumer has the right to be treated with dignity and respect, to have personal property respected, to be free from abuse, neglect and exploitation, to take part in planning services, and to complain without fear of retaliation. Complaints may be made to the Agency or to the Pennsylvania Department of Health hotline at 1-866-254-5164.

Professional boundaries. You must follow the consumer’s service plan and perform only the tasks it lists. You must not accept money, gifts, loans or tips; borrow from or lend to a consumer; take or use a consumer’s belongings; be named in a consumer’s will; act as power of attorney or guardian; endorse or cash a consumer’s checks; use a consumer’s card, bank account or passwords; or give medical advice or medication beyond what the service plan allows. Report any request to do these things to your supervisor.

Abuse, neglect and exploitation. If you suspect a consumer is being abused, neglected, exploited or abandoned, you must report it. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911 first. Then tell your supervisor or the Administrator right away and make the verbal report required by Agency policy, followed by the written report form. Reports about older adults go to the Area Agency on Aging; reports about children go to ChildLine at 1-800-932-0313. Do not investigate on your own or confront the suspected person. Good-faith reports are protected by law and retaliation is prohibited. Failing to report is itself a violation.

Billing integrity. The Agency bills Medicaid, managed care organizations and other payers only for services actually provided. Never record or sign for services that were not delivered and never help anyone submit a false claim. Concerns may be reported to the Administrator.

Illegal remuneration and referrals. The Agency does not pay, give or accept anything of value in exchange for referrals, and employees may not solicit consumers or referrals for personal gain. Claims of improper solicitation will be investigated, and employees in violation will be subject to discipline up to termination.

6.0 Separation from Employment

6.1 Resignation. As an at-will employee not subject to an employment contract, you may end your employment at any time, with or without good cause. You are requested to give written notice to your supervisor at least two (2) weeks before your resignation takes effect, to assist the Agency in planning for your departure.

6.2 Dismissal. As an at-will employee not subject to an employment contract, the Agency may end your employment at any time and for any reason or no reason, regardless of your work performance or compliance with this Manual.

6.2.1 Automatic Dismissal. The commission of any offense considered serious enough by management, without limitation to those listed here, will, except in extraordinary circumstances, be followed by immediate dismissal: (A) making false statements or omitting pertinent facts on an employment application or in an interview; (B) abuse, neglect or exploitation of a consumer; (C) threatening, assaulting, fighting with or harassing any employee, consumer or other person; (D) stealing or deliberately damaging Agency, consumer or employee property; (E) disclosing consumer or Agency confidential information; (F) possessing a weapon at work; (G) reporting to work under the influence of alcohol, narcotics or other drugs, unless prescribed by a physician; (H) falsifying or destroying Agency documents, visit records, timesheets or computer files; (I) conviction of a felony and/or imprisonment, or loss of a required clearance; (J) taking unauthorized leave or failing to show up for work for more than three (3) consecutive days without notifying a supervisor; and (K) transporting unauthorized passengers in an Agency vehicle. Civil and/or criminal penalties or prosecution may also apply.

6.3 Return of Property. Upon termination, voluntary or involuntary, you must immediately return all files, records, keys, identification badges, uniforms, tools and any other materials or confidential information belonging to the Agency. Unless prohibited by law, final pay will not be made until Agency property is returned in satisfactory condition; the cost of replacing items not returned may be deducted from your final pay where permitted by law.

You are entitled to express your point of view on work-related matters in a constructive manner, and to make productive suggestions through any communication channel available at appropriate times and places.
