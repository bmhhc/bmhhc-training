AO.004 General Services Overview

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. provides non-medical home care services to consumers in their own homes. Services are delivered by trained direct care workers under 28 Pa. Code Chapter 611. The agency does not provide skilled nursing, therapy or other medical care.

Purpose

To state what the agency does and does not do, so consumers, families and referral sources know what to expect.

Procedure

  1. Services provided. Personal care (bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, eating, moving about, and help with medicine the consumer takes themselves), help with household tasks, companionship, respite care, and other nonskilled care set out in the consumer's service plan.
  2. Who we serve. Consumers of any age, regardless of sex, income, race, national origin, color, religion, ancestry, handicap or disability.
  3. Offices. Each branch keeps its own hours, phone number and service area on file.

AO.006 Agency Administration, Supervision, Management and Oversight

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. has a Board of Directors that functions as the governing body and has full legal authority and responsibility for the operation of the agency. The Board appoints an Agency Administrator, who is responsible for agency management and oversight and for employing a care supervisor who is available during normal agency operating hours (Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.).

Purpose

To state who is responsible for running the agency and for supervising direct care workers.

Procedure

  1. The Board of Directors is responsible for:
    • • Appointing a qualified Administrator of Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc.
    • • Adopting and annually reviewing the Board of Directors bylaws.
    • • Meeting at least once per year to oversee the management and fiscal affairs of the agency, including approving the annual budget.
  2. The Administrator is responsible for the overall organization, planning, delivery and evaluation of the agency's services, and keeps ongoing communication among the Board of Directors, agency staff and the care supervisor.
  3. The Administrator ensures that:
    • • Qualified personnel are employed, using methods such as online job postings, newspaper advertising and referrals from current staff.
    • • Staff receive adequate education, training and evaluations.
    • • Public information materials are accurate.
    • • An effective budgeting and accounting system is kept in place.
  4. The care supervisor oversees the day-to-day work of direct care workers, including the development of worker qualifications, the assignment of workers to consumers, and follow-up on consumer and family concerns.
  5. After hours, on weekends and on holidays (including 4:30 p.m. to 9:00 a.m. each day), the on-call system described in AO.009 is used so that a supervisor can always be reached. The on-call schedule is updated regularly based on the availability of qualified supervisory staff and the needs of consumers.
  6. Delegation of authority: in the absence of the Administrator, the care supervisor, or another person the Board names in writing, acts for the Administrator.
  7. Any change in the Administrator, ownership or governing body is reported within thirty (30) days to the Pennsylvania Department of Health and to any other agency or payer that requires notice, including UPMC and the Office of Long-Term Living. AO.009 On-Call Course of Action

Policy

It is the policy of this agency that consumers, families and direct care workers can reach a supervisor after hours to ask questions, report a problem or arrange coverage.

Purpose

To make sure someone is always available when a consumer's care is affected outside normal office hours.

Procedure

  1. The on-call system is not an emergency response system. Consumers and families are told at the start of services, and again as needed, to call 911 for any emergency and to call the on-call number for schedule or care questions.
  2. After hours, on weekends and on holidays, calls are answered by the answering system, which only routes the call. The on-call supervisor decides how serious each call is and how it will be handled.
  3. The on-call supervisor returns calls promptly and decides whether to send a replacement worker, call the consumer's family or emergency contact, or contact emergency services.
  4. If a consumer is admitted to the hospital or dies, the on-call supervisor notifies any worker scheduled to visit so that no unnecessary visits are made.
  5. If a worker cannot cover a shift, the on-call supervisor arranges a back-up worker (see AO.010) or notifies the consumer and the care supervisor first thing the next business day.
  6. The on-call supervisor records each call, the action taken and any follow-up needed, and gives the record to the care supervisor the next business day.
  7. The Administrator or care supervisor may change the on-call procedure as needed to meet consumers' needs.

AO.014 Communicating with Visual, Hearing or Speech Impaired Consumers

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. provides services to consumers who are visually, hearing or speech impaired. Needed services and accommodations are provided at no charge to the consumer or family.

Purpose

To make sure consumers with a visual, hearing or speech impairment are not denied effective notice of benefits, waivers of rights or consent to services.

Procedure

  1. At first contact, intake staff discuss the consumer's communication limits and needs with the consumer and the referring agency or person, so arrangements are ready before services start.
  2. Visual impairment: staff read aloud every document normally given to a consumer to be sure it is understood. Support services are available through the Pennsylvania Association for the Blind, (717) 766-2020.
  3. Hearing impairment: at the in-person assessment the care supervisor or designee identifies how the consumer hears best and which communication methods and aids the consumer prefers (paper and pencil, American Sign Language, Signed Exact English, lip reading, a communication board, gestures), and any hearing devices the consumer uses.
  4. If a consumer uses sign language as the primary means of communication, the agency provides a qualified interpreter at no cost for both spoken and written information. Interpreters can be arranged through ASL Services, (717) 755-3212.
  5. Family members and friends are not used as interpreters unless the consumer has been told that a qualified interpreter is available at no cost and, without any pressure, chooses a family member or friend.
  6. The consumer's choice of interpreter, and the presence of an interpreter, is documented after every visit.
  7. The consumer's service plan identifies the preferred communication method, and the care supervisor shares it with all direct care workers who serve the consumer.
  8. The consumer and family receive in writing the services that will be provided, who will provide them, their rights as they relate to the service plan, and how to reach the agency.
  9. Direct care workers may use flashcards for basic tasks such as bathing, grooming, dressing, housekeeping and meal preparation. Flashcards are not used when reviewing important documents or consent forms.

AO.019 Regulations Compliance Implementation

Policy

It is the policy of the agency to:

  1. Implement and enforce all state and federal regulations that apply to agency operation and management, at the main office and any branch office.
  2. Comply with the regulations on Rights of the Elderly and consumer rights and responsibilities.
  3. Promote each consumer's interest and well-being by telling the consumer of his or her rights, in writing and orally.
  4. Protect and promote the rights and civil liberties of each person served by the agency.
  5. Tell each consumer of his or her responsibilities.

Purpose

To make sure state and federal requirements are followed and that any shortfall found is corrected through a Corrective Action Plan (CAP).

Procedure

  1. The care supervisor or designee gives each consumer written and verbal information on rights, in a language the consumer can understand, before services begin.
  2. The care supervisor or designee reviews consumer rights and responsibilities with the consumer. Rights of the Elderly are reviewed with every consumer aged 60 or older.
  3. The consumer or representative signs an acknowledgment of receipt. The original goes in the client record at the agency and a copy goes in the consumer's home folder.
  4. Updates to consumer rights and responsibilities are reviewed with staff when changes occur.
  5. The Department will monitor the agency at least once every two years. Monitoring may be announced or unannounced.
  6. The agency will submit documentation requested by the Department showing compliance with: (a) the Medical Assistance provider agreement, including the waiver addendum; (b) all Medical Assistance regulations and policies that apply to home care; (c) the approved waiver and any approved amendments; and (d) any state or federal requirement.
  7. If the Department issues a written statement of findings, the agency responds with a CAP, on the Department's form, when requested.
  8. The CAP must include: the agency's name and address; its Medical Assistance identification number; the action steps for each finding and the dates they will be completed; how the steps will correct the finding and the date the finding will be corrected; and the signature of the agency showing it will carry out the CAP.
  9. The Department reviews and monitors the CAP to make sure each finding is corrected. It may reject a CAP and ask the agency to revise it, or write a CAP for the agency to carry out. The agency carries out any CAP the Department approves.
  10. The Department may conduct a follow-up review to confirm the agency is carrying out the CAP.

AO.024 Illegal Solicitation and Remuneration

Policy

Individuals employed by or under contract with the agency shall not willfully or knowingly solicit, offer, receive or pay any remuneration, including bribes, rebates or kickbacks, directly or indirectly, openly or secretly, in cash or in kind, to induce the referral of consumers whose care is paid for under any federal or state health care program.

Purpose

To prevent illegal payments for referrals.

Procedure

  1. All employees and contractors are told of this policy at orientation.
  2. Employees and contractors sign, with title and date, to show they have been instructed on the policy.
  3. Violation of this policy is grounds for immediate termination, civil suit, or a formal report to the appropriate authority.
  4. Federal and state health care programs include, but are not limited to, Medical Assistance (Medicaid, Title XIX), including home and community-based waiver programs, and other programs named in 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b.
  5. Advertising is not prohibited, except advertising that is false, misleading or deceptive.
  6. Payment for advertising, marketing or other services is not prohibited if it is set in advance, is consistent with the fair market value of the services, and is not based on the volume or value of referrals.
  7. This policy does not prohibit any payment or business arrangement that is not prohibited by 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b(b) or its regulations.
  8. Illegal remuneration is a felony and, on conviction, is punishable by a fine, imprisonment, or both.

Section Two: Human Resources

HR.001 Employee Hiring / Competency Requirements

Policy

Every direct care worker must meet the hiring and competency requirements below before being assigned to a consumer, and a confidential personnel record is kept for every employee.

Purpose

To set the minimum hiring and competency steps and to make sure the agency keeps the records that show they were met.

Procedure

  1. At hiring or rostering of a direct care worker, the agency will: (a) hold a face-to-face interview; (b) obtain at least two satisfactory references that confirm the person's ability to provide home care services; and (c) complete a criminal background check and, if applicable, a ChildLine child abuse clearance as required by 28 Pa. Code 611.52 and 611.53 (see HR.002).
  2. Personnel records are confidential. They are kept in a locked file in the Human Resources file cabinet and kept current.
  3. Each personnel record contains: the application, interview notes and references; the signed job description; criminal history and child abuse clearance results; a current copy of CPR certification if required for the position; a copy of the driver's license and vehicle insurance if the employee drives for the agency; tuberculosis screening results (see HR.009); signed statements on safety and health, illegal remuneration, abuse and neglect reporting, infection control and confidentiality; the signed orientation form; and competency evaluations and tests.
  4. Form I-9 and medical information are kept in a separate folder. Wage records are kept in a separate confidential file.
  5. Personnel files are kept for at least five years after the employee leaves, unless a longer period is required.
  6. Competency: before a direct care worker is assigned to a consumer, the agency makes sure the worker has done one of the following: (a) holds a valid nurse's license in Pennsylvania; (b) passed a competency test developed by the agency; or (c) completed a training program developed by the agency or another entity that meets state regulation, a home health aide training program that meets 42 CFR 484.36, a nurse aide program approved by the Pennsylvania Department of Education, or a training program that meets the standards of a Medicaid waiver or other publicly funded home and community-based program the agency takes part in.
  7. The agency keeps documentation in the worker's file that it has reviewed the worker's competency to perform assigned duties through direct observation, testing, training, consumer feedback or another method approved by the Department, or a combination of methods. The review is repeated at least once a year after initial competency is shown.
  8. Each employee reads and signs that he or she understands the policies on safety and health, confidentiality and infection control. The signed form goes in the personnel file.

HR.009 Employee Health Screening

Policy

Every direct care worker who will have contact with consumers must be screened for tuberculosis (TB) before that contact, and the screening must be updated every 12 months, as required by 28 Pa. Code 611.56.

Purpose

To protect consumers and workers from the spread of TB and to keep a record of each worker's screening.

Procedure

  1. Screening follows CDC guidelines. Before first contact with a consumer, each worker has a TB test (a two-step skin test, or a blood test) or provides proof of a negative test within the past year.
  2. Known positive reactors provide a copy of a previous chest x-ray if available and complete a TB symptom questionnaire every year. A chest x-ray with follow-up is required if the worker has symptoms.
  3. Skin tests must be read by a licensed health care provider 48 to 72 hours after they are given, or they are not valid and must be repeated. The provider signs the form, and the signed form goes in the employee's confidential health file.
  4. Workers are told of any abnormal result. A positive result requires follow-up and treatment as the health care provider directs.
  5. A worker who tests positive is not scheduled for consumer contact until the worker's health care provider gives a written release to work.
  6. Each worker provides updated screening documentation at least every 12 months, counted from the date of the last screening. The care supervisor tracks the due dates.

CLN.006 Missed Visits and Schedule Changes

Policy

When a scheduled visit or shift is missed or changed, the agency finds coverage if it can and tells the people who need to know.

Purpose

To protect consumers who depend on scheduled care. This replaces the physician-notification rule.

Procedure

  1. A worker who cannot work a shift, or who finds the consumer is not home, calls the supervisor right away.
  2. The supervisor arranges a replacement worker where possible and tells the consumer or family.
  3. If no one can cover, the supervisor tells the consumer or family and, as the payer requires, the service coordinator or managed care plan.
  4. The missed visit is recorded with its reason (worker unavailable, consumer cancelled, consumer in hospital, weather, other). EVV exceptions are completed as the system requires.
  5. The Administrator reviews missed visits each quarter and acts on any pattern (QAPI.001).

CLN.008 Consumer Rights and Responsibilities

Policy

It is the policy of the agency to follow all state and federal rules on consumer rights, to tell each consumer of his or her rights and responsibilities in writing and orally, and to protect the rights and civil liberties of every person the agency serves.

Purpose

To make sure each consumer knows what to expect from the agency and what the agency expects of the consumer.

Procedure

  1. Before services begin, the care supervisor or designee gives each consumer, or the consumer's representative, written and verbal information on rights in a language the consumer can understand, and reviews it with the consumer.
  2. The consumer has the right to: take part in service planning and receive services that reasonably fit individual needs and preferences, except where the safety of the direct care worker is at risk; at least 10 calendar days' written notice before the agency ends services (see CLN.034); be treated with respect and have property treated with care; have personal and client records kept confidential; refuse services and be told the results; and voice complaints and grievances without fear of reprisal (see QAPI.005).
  3. No one affiliated with the agency may take power of attorney or guardianship over a consumer, and the agency may not require a consumer to endorse checks over to the agency.
  4. The consumer is expected to: help plan his or her services; ask questions when something is not understood; provide a safe home for staff (no smoking while staff are present, pets restrained as needed, no visible firearms or illegal drugs); tell the agency if a visit cannot take place or if needs change; and treat staff with respect.
  5. The consumer or representative signs an acknowledgment that this information was received. The original goes in the client record and a copy in the consumer's home folder.
  6. Updates to consumer rights and responsibilities are reviewed with staff when changes occur.

CLN.010 Service Plan

Policy

Every consumer has a written service plan that the consumer helped develop.

To make sure services fit the consumer's needs and preferences (28 Pa. Code 611.57(a)(1)). This replaces the physician plan of care.

CLN.018 Reporting Suspected Abuse, Neglect and Exploitation

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. prohibits abuse, neglect and exploitation of consumers. The agency will begin an investigation immediately of every known or alleged act by an agency employee, contractor or volunteer, as soon as the act is witnessed or the allegation is received.

Purpose

To prevent abuse, neglect and exploitation, to report suspected cases to the proper authorities, and to protect and support consumers.

Procedure

  1. Abuse includes any physical injury not caused by accident, sexual contact or harassment, financial exploitation, restraining or humiliating a consumer, and withholding regularly scheduled meals. Neglect is failure to provide care or services that could cause actual harm or serious risk of harm. Emotional abuse is harm to a person's ability to think or reason or to feel normal emotions.
  2. Any employee who suspects abuse, neglect or exploitation reports it immediately. Call 911 if the person is in a life-threatening situation, and take action to keep the person safe.
  3. Report to the Administrator or care supervisor, who starts the initial investigation, and to the consumer's service coordinator.
  4. Report to the proper authority: the Pennsylvania Department of Aging Protective Services hotline, 1- 800-490-8505, for adults; ChildLine, 1-800-932-0313, if the person is under 18; and the Department of Health home care complaint line, 1-866-254-5164. [Confirm these numbers before printing.]
  5. The employee completes the required incident report and enters it in the Pennsylvania Enterprise Incident Management (EIM) system when the program requires it (see QAPI.003). Use the attached ANE Verbal Report form to record each call.
  6. The care supervisor decides whether the consumer's service plan needs to change.
  7. Staff may discuss the matter with the family after the authorities finish their review, and only with the Administrator's knowledge.
  8. All employees are trained on identifying and reporting suspected abuse and neglect within 30 days of hire and every year after. Training is recorded in the employee's file.
  9. Reports and contacts are documented and kept in a separate confidential file. This policy is reviewed every year.

CLN.034 Ending Services

Policy

The agency ends services only for good reason and gives written notice first, as 28 Pa. Code 611.57 requires.

Purpose

To protect consumers from sudden loss of care. This replaces the 2013 discharge policy, which gave 5 days' notice and referred to physicians, OASIS and homebound status.

Procedure

  1. Reasons services may end: the consumer asks to stop; the consumer moves out of the service area; the consumer no longer needs the service or the authorization ends; funding is no longer available; the consumer does not pay; the consumer needs a higher level of care; no qualified worker is available; the home is unsafe; the consumer or family abuses staff; or the consumer repeatedly refuses to follow the agreed service plan.
  2. Notice. The agency gives the consumer or representative at least 10 calendar days' advance written notice before ending services.
  3. Shorter notice is allowed only when the consumer has not paid despite notice and is more than 14 days behind, or when a worker's health or safety is at risk. The agency documents its efforts to reach the consumer and family.
  4. Immediate end may also occur at the consumer's request, when the consumer needs emergency care, or during a disaster that puts health and safety at risk.
  5. The supervisor helps the consumer find another provider and tells the service coordinator or managed care plan.
  6. The supervisor writes a closing note in the client record: the reason, the date and the help given. The record is kept as IM.005 requires.

Section Four: Quality Assurance and Performance Improvement

QAPI.001 Quality Assurance and Performance Improvement Program

The agency checks the quality of its services on a regular schedule and acts on what POLICY: it finds.

Purpose

To keep care safe and consumers satisfied. The goal is a consumer satisfaction rate of 95 percent or higher. This replaces the Professional Advisory Committee and Medicare outcome language.

Procedure

1. The Administrator oversees the program and decides what to measure, what to study, and what to change.

  1. The program follows a cycle: measure, plan a fix, put it in place, then check that it worked.
  2. Areas of focus: access to services; consumer rights; service delivery and the service plan; continuity of care; records; infection control; the home environment; incidents; and complaints.
  3. Each branch runs a consumer satisfaction survey at least once a year and reports its results separately (see the Services Satisfaction Survey).
  4. Each quarter the Administrator reviews incidents (QAPI.003), complaints (QAPI.005), missed visits (CLN.006) and falls (CLN.031) for patterns.
  5. Client records are reviewed each quarter (QAPI.002).
  6. Contract services are reviewed at least once a year.
  7. When a problem needs focused work, the Administrator names a small team with a leader and a task. The team keeps notes and reports back.
  8. The Administrator holds a program evaluation at least once a year (AO.007) and reports results to the owners [or governing body].

QAPI.003 Incident Reporting and Serious Events

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. will keep a system for reporting incidents POLICY: and following up with corrective action. The purposes of the incident report are to find problems early, to start early investigation of serious events, to build a record that helps find and correct long-term problems, and to cross-check with other risk systems. The incident report is not part of the client record. The incident reporting system is part of the agency's quality assurance program (QAPI.001).

Purpose

To make sure every incident involving a consumer or worker is reported, reviewed and, where needed, corrected.

Procedure

1. An incident is an unusual event involving a consumer or worker that was unintended, undesirable or unexpected. Reportable incidents include, but are not limited to: injury or fall; abuse, neglect or exploitation; death; hospitalization; a missing consumer; a missed visit that puts a consumer at risk; a worker injury; a motor vehicle accident while on agency business; unsafe conditions in the home; theft or damage to property; and threats of harm.

  1. The person who finds or learns of the incident tells the care supervisor immediately, follows up with the consumer and family as the care supervisor directs, and keeps the information confidential.
  2. The person completes an incident report within 24 hours if possible. The care supervisor or Administrator may help. The report records the consumer and worker involved, date, time and location, witnesses, a description of what happened, any injuries, who was notified and when, and the follow-up action taken.
  3. Serious incidents are also reported through the Pennsylvania Enterprise Incident Management (EIM) system, or to the consumer's service coordinator, within the time the program requires. [Confirm the reporting times in your UPMC and Office of Long-Term Living incident management requirements.]

Section Five: Safety & Infection Control

S/IC.012 Emergency Preparedness / Disaster Plan

Policy

Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. is committed to providing needed services to consumers during emergencies, whether caused by weather, a civil disturbance or another event. The agency's goal is to continue services with as little interruption as possible while protecting the safety of staff and consumers. The Administrator or care supervisor is told of all emergencies and is responsible for activating this plan.

Purpose

To keep consumers safe and services going during an emergency, and to meet the back-up and emergency planning requirements of the agency's waiver and payer agreements.

Procedure

  1. Staff notification: when the plan is activated, the Administrator or care supervisor starts a phone calling chain to reach all staff. Staff keep an updated list of staff phone numbers. If all agency phones fail, staff listen to the Emergency Broadcast System. Staff may be asked to take on different duties during an emergency.
  2. Consumer notification and priority: at the start of services, the care supervisor identifies consumers who would be at high risk in an emergency and reviews the emergency plan with them and their caregivers. Each consumer is assigned a priority level (see 10). If an emergency lasts more than a day, the agency tries to contact consumers daily to check on their status and needs.
  3. Continuation of services: the care supervisor keeps an up-to-date list of active consumers with name, address and phone number, checks which staff are available given where the emergency is, and reassigns workers using the priority list. If an emergency prevents services, the Administrator may ask local, county or state emergency services for help, and may arrange alternatives such as another agency. Vendors providing critical supplies are expected to have their own emergency plan.
  4. Restoration: the Administrator decides when the emergency is over. The care supervisor contacts staff and consumers to find current needs. Handwritten documentation is turned in to the office within forty-eight (48) hours of the return to normal operations.
  5. Office emergencies: for fire, bomb threat or gas leak, the person who finds the threat announces the need to evacuate and calls 911. Staff leave by the posted exit plan and meet at the designated spot outside. The Administrator or designee checks that everyone is out, and staff do not go back in until told it is safe. Staff may use a fire extinguisher on a small fire. For a power failure, staff stay put and the electric company and landlord are called; if power is off for more than an hour the office closes and the on-call procedure (AO.009) is used. For a phone failure, the phone company is called and the Administrator decides whether to call consumers by cell phone or forward the office lines. Computer data is backed up each business day and a copy is kept off-site.
  6. Loss of records: duplicate computer backup files are kept securely off-site. Paper records are kept in locked cabinets. Lost or destroyed records are recreated from backups.
  7. Employee emergencies: if a worker is unable to continue a visit, the care supervisor decides which consumers still need service and reassigns them using the priority list. Rescheduled visits are documented. For a temporary staffing shortage, the Administrator or care supervisor contacts other staff, assigns management staff to visit if appropriate, and prioritizes visits. For a critical shortage, the consumer or family is told, offered the choice of moving to another agency, and the referral source and service coordinator are told.
  8. Weather and community-wide emergencies: the Administrator makes all decisions about operations. The weather service and local emergency management alerts are monitored. The calling chain is used, visits are prioritized, consumers are told of changes, and families are shown what to do in advance when possible. Canceled visits are documented and the service coordinator is told.
  9. Communication and training: the Administrator is the only spokesperson with the press and distributes a new emergency contact plan every year. The plan is covered in new staff orientation and reviewed with all staff every year. Building evacuation drills are held at least once a year, and fire safety training including hands-on use of a fire extinguisher is held at least every two (2) years.
  10. Consumer priority listing: the care supervisor assigns every consumer a level. High (first priority): the consumer lives alone with no family or caregiver support, would be harmed if care were missed, or needs daily or timed services. These consumers are visited if it is safe. Moderate: no significant harm is expected if services are postponed one or two days. A capable caregiver may be coached by phone, and the consumer is called and visited if possible. Low: services can be postponed without harm, and the consumer is called. If the emergency makes it unsafe for staff, visits are not made.

S/IC.016 Infection Control Program Overview

Policy

The agency follows CDC, OSHA and Pennsylvania infection control rules.

Purpose

To protect consumers, workers and families from infection.

Procedure

  4. Education. Workers teach consumers and families hand washing, safe disposal of contaminated items, protective equipment, and early signs of infection.
  5. Control. A worker who is sick reports to the supervisor. The supervisor decides on time off or work limits using CDC guidance and, where needed, a health care provider's advice.
  6. The Administrator reports results to the owners at least once a year.

S/IC.021 Exposure Control Plan

Policy

The agency keeps a program to make sure that all exposures to blood or body substances are reported promptly and managed according to the guidelines of the Centers for Disease Control (CDC) and the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA).

Purpose

To protect employees from blood-borne pathogens and to meet the OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard.

Procedure

  1. Exposure determination: direct care workers who provide bathing and personal care are expected to have exposure (Class I). Office staff are not expected to have exposure (Class III). The Administrator reviews these classes whenever job duties change.
  2. All staff use standard infection control and universal precautions. Engineering and work practice controls to reduce exposure include: leak-proof containers for contaminated items and protective equipment; hand washing before and after consumer care and before and after wearing gloves, with liquid soap, alcohol-based sanitizer and paper towels provided; contaminated sharps are never bent, recapped or broken and are disposed of in a sharps container; and equipment is cleaned after each use with germicide, bleach or alcohol.
  3. Personal protective equipment is provided at no cost to the employee and chosen for the exposure expected. It must not let blood or other infectious material reach the employee's clothing, skin, eyes, mouth or mucous membranes. Workers carry the supply they need. Contaminated equipment is removed as soon as possible and placed in a plastic bag in a garbage receptacle. Clean equipment is removed and discarded before leaving the consumer's home. Laundry is handled by universal precautions.
  4. Hepatitis B vaccine: employees who could be exposed to blood are offered the vaccine within 10 days of starting work. Employees who already have the vaccine or decline it sign a waiver, and may ask for the vaccine later. The Administrator or care supervisor makes sure the vaccine is offered and the waivers are signed.
  5. After an exposure: the employee reports it to the Administrator or care supervisor immediately and completes the exposure incident form. The employee is sent promptly to a licensed health care provider for evaluation. When indicated, the consumer or family is asked for consent to test the source blood and to release HIV-related information. If the consumer refuses, the refusal is documented in writing. The evaluating provider certifies the exposure, orders testing and decides what follow-up is needed. The provider's written report goes in the employee's confidential medical file. The agency pays for source and employee testing and consultation fees.
  6. Training: all staff are trained at orientation and every year on the OSHA bloodborne pathogens standard, how blood-borne diseases spread, and this plan.
  7. Employee medical records are kept separately from personnel files by the Administrator, for the duration of employment plus the period OSHA requires. Each record holds the employee's name, hepatitis B vaccine consent or declination and date of vaccination, and, after an exposure, the exposure report, consent forms, evaluation, test results and follow-up. Only the Administrator, approved supervisory staff and the affected employee have access.

IM.001 Release of Client Information

Policy

The client record is kept for the benefit of the consumer and the agency and is the property of Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. The consumer has a legal right to the information in the record, subject to legal limits such as those for minors or for people found legally incompetent. Information is released for any purpose other than the consumer's services only with the express written authorization of the consumer or the consumer's legal representative, or as the law requires.

Purpose

To protect the confidentiality of consumer information and to follow HIPAA and Pennsylvania law on release of records.

Procedure

  1. All information in a client record is confidential. Records of current consumers are not reviewed or copied by anyone not connected with the consumer's services unless the consumer gives permission.
  2. All requests for client information are sent to the Administrator or care supervisor for processing.
  3. A consumer may review his or her record and ask for corrections if the consumer believes information is wrong. A consumer may obtain a copy for a reasonable fee.
  4. An authorization to release information must be in writing and contain: the agency's name and address; the person or organization the information goes to; the consumer's name; the purpose and the specific information to be released; the time period covered; and the signature and date. (An Authorization for Use or Disclosure of Protected Health Information form is attached.)
  5. Faxed information is preceded by an agency cover sheet showing the requester's name, fax and telephone number. A copy of the fax is filed in the client record. Information is faxed only to providers or agencies involved in the consumer's services.
  6. HIV-related information: this information may be released only with a specific written HIV disclosure authorization from the consumer, to the consumer after a signed disclosure authorization, under a valid court order (a subpoena alone is not enough), or as the Pennsylvania Confidentiality of HIV-Related Information Act otherwise allows.
  7. A consumer's information may also be shared without authorization with the state or a payer as required to oversee, monitor or pay for services, and as required by law, such as to report suspected abuse or neglect.
  8. Requests from attorneys, courts or law enforcement are sent to the Administrator before anything is released.

IM.005 Confidentiality, Protection and Retention of Client Records

Policy

The client record is the property of Blue Mountain Home Health Care, Inc. and is not removed from the office except as this policy allows. Closed records are kept in a locked, secure area of the agency office. All staff and contractors protect each consumer's privacy by keeping all demographic, identifying and service information confidential, and sign a confidentiality statement before starting work. Information about the HIPAA privacy rule and the agency's confidentiality practices is given to all employees and consumers.

Purpose

To protect consumer information from unauthorized use and to keep records for the required time.

Procedure

  1. Contents: the contents of the client record are described in IM.003. A record may include electronic information, notes of telephone communications, and faxed information.
  2. Confidentiality: information about a consumer is not released without the consumer's written permission, except as IM.001 and the law allow. Records of participants in Medicaid waiver programs are confidential and are not open to anyone without the participant's written consent or a court order, other than: the participant; the participant's legal guardian; provider staff providing a service to the participant; an agent of the Department; and a person holding the participant's health care power of attorney or proxy.
